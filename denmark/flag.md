# Flag of Denmark

The Dannebrog, meaning "cloth of the Danes," holds the record as the oldest continuously used national flag. According to legend, the flag fell from heaven in 1219 during King Valdemar II's battle near Tallinn, Estonia. The red field with white Nordic cross represents Christianity.

Red symbolizes bravery and strength, while white represents peace and honesty. For centuries a royal privilege, the flag became popular after the 1848 First Schleswig War when Danish soldiers returned home to find red-and-white flags adorning private residences. June 15 is celebrated as Valdemar's Day, commemorating the legendary divine gift and the flag's enduring role in Danish cultural identity.
