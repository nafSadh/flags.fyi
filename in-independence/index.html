The national flag of India, colloquially called Tiraṅgā, is a horizontal rectangular tricolour flag, the colours being of India saffron, white and India green; with the Ashoka Chakra, a 24-spoke wheel, in navy blue at its centre. It was adopted in its present form during a meeting of the Constituent Assembly held on 22 July 1947, and it became the official flag of the Union of India on 15 August 1947. The flag was subsequently retained as that of the Republic of India. In India, the term "tricolour" almost always refers to the Indian national flag. read more ↗

Flag of the Indian Independence Movement

Description

  • The Indian independence movement flag (1931 version) featured saffron, white, and green horizontal stripes with a spinning wheel (charkha) centred — Mahatma Gandhi's symbol of self-reliance
  • Adopted by the Indian National Congress in 1931, this flag evolved from earlier versions
  • At independence in 1947, the charkha was replaced by the Ashoka Chakra to create the current national flag
  • Construction: three equal horizontal bands of saffron (top), white, and green. A blue spinning wheel (charkha) centred on the white stripe. Ratio 2:3

Flag of the Indian Independence Movement

The Indian independence movement flag (1931 version) featured saffron, white, and green horizontal stripes with a spinning wheel (charkha) centred — Mahatma Gandhi's symbol of self-reliance. Adopted by the Indian National Congress in 1931, this flag evolved from earlier versions.

India's independence flag (1947) represented freedom from British rule. Designed by Pingali Venkayya. The modern Indian flag is based on this independence flag.
