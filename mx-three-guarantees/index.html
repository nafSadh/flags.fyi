The Mexican Empire was a constitutional monarchy and the first independent government of Mexico. It was also the only former viceroyalty of the Spanish Empire to establish a monarchy after gaining independence. The empire existed from 1821 to 1823, making it one of the few modern-era independent monarchies in the Americas. To distinguish it from the later Second Mexican Empire (1864–1867) under Emperor Maximilian, this historical period is commonly referred to as the First Mexican Empire. The empire was led by former Royal Spanish military officer Agustín de Iturbide, who ruled as Agustín I. read more ↗

Flag of the Three Guarantees

Description

  • The Flag of the Three Guarantees (1821) was Mexico's first national flag, featuring diagonal stripes of green, white, and red with a crowned eagle on a cactus in the centre and three stars
  • The three guarantees were: independence from Spain, Roman Catholic religion, and union of all inhabitants
  • These were the principles of the Plan of Iguala that secured Mexican independence
  • Construction: three diagonal stripes of white (top-left to bottom-right), green (behind), and red (front)
  • Three six-pointed stars in each stripe
  • A crowned eagle on a cactus centred
  • Ratio approximately 4:7

Flag of the Three Guarantees

The Flag of the Three Guarantees (1821) was Mexico's first national flag, featuring diagonal stripes of green, white, and red with a crowned eagle on a cactus in the centre and three stars. The three guarantees were: independence from Spain, Roman Catholic religion, and union of all inhabitants.

The flag of the Army of Three Guarantees represented Mexico's independence movement. The three guarantees were religion, independence, and union. Led to Mexico's independence from Spain in 1821.
